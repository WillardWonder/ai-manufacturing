01

Watch it work.

Every shop has a Dale: 31 years on Press 4 and the only one who really knows the die change. Here's how AI gets that out of his head and onto the floor.

Start with the expert, not the keyboard.

Dale talked through his die change while someone recorded it on a phone. Most phones turn a voice memo into text for free.

Dale's voice memo, word for word

"Okay so first thing, you lock it out, obviously, main disconnect and the air. Then I always run the slide down to bottom dead center before I pull anything. Some guys don't and that's how you get pinched. Pull the old die, use the cart, not the forklift, the forklift guys always ding the shoes. Clean the bolster, I mean really clean it, one chip under there and your parts are out. Set the new die, line up the locating pins, then the clamps. Snug them, then torque them, there's a number on the sheet in the office. Shut height, you check that before you cycle, every time. Then inch it through once, then one part, then you call quality for first article before you run. Oh, and the scrap chute, swap it if you're going from the four-inch brackets to the long ones."

This part isn't AI.The knowledge comes from a person. AI's job is to turn it into something everyone can use.

Ask vaguely, get something generic.

Write an SOP for a die change.

What came back

1. Purpose: This procedure ensures safe and efficient die changes in accordance with company standards. 2. Scope: Applies to all stamping presses. 3. Responsibilities: The Production Manager shall ensure all personnel are trained... 4. Procedure: Ensure proper PPE is worn. Remove the die using appropriate lifting equipment...

It filled in every blank.None of it is Dale's. It says "appropriate lifting equipment" when Dale said use the cart, never the forklift. It doesn't know your press, your people, or your rules.

Say who it's for, what it should look like, and what it can't do.

Turn the transcript below into a one-page work instruction for the Press 4 die change. Who it's for: operators with less than a year of experience. Some read English as a second language. Format: numbered steps, one action per step, short sentences. Safety steps first, marked SAFETY. Rules: Use only what's in the transcript. Don't add steps he didn't say. If anything is missing or unclear, list it under "Questions for the supervisor" instead of guessing. Transcript: [Dale's voice memo]

No magic words.The audience, the format, and a rule against making things up. Context only you have.

A draft that's actually Dale's.

PRESS 4 DIE CHANGE: WORK INSTRUCTION SAFETY 1. Lock out the main disconnect and the air supply. SAFETY 2. Run the slide down to bottom dead center before removing anything. 3. Remove the old die with the die cart. Do not use the forklift. 4. Clean the bolster completely. One chip can put parts out of spec. 5. Set the new die. Line up the locating pins. 6. Snug the clamps. Then torque them to the value on the setup sheet. 7. Check shut height before cycling the press. 8. Inch the press through one full stroke. 9. Run one part. Call Quality for first-article inspection before running production. 10. If changing from 4-inch brackets to long brackets, swap the scrap chute. Questions for the supervisor - What is the clamp torque value, and where is the setup sheet kept? - What is the correct shut height for each die? - Does lockout include any other energy sources, such as hydraulic or stored pressure?

Every step traces back to Dale.The gaps became questions instead of guesses, including one about other energy sources a supervisor needs to answer.

Build on it in the same conversation.

Keep the steps exactly as written. Add a Spanish version below it.
Now write a five-question check a supervisor can use to confirm a new operator understands this.

One conversation, three pieces.A work instruction, a translation, and a training check. A fluent speaker still reviews the translation, especially the safety steps.

A person checks it, finishes it, and signs it.

Dale and his supervisor answer the three questions, add the torque value and shut heights, have the Spanish checked, then sign, date, and post it at the press.

  • Every step matches how the job is really done
  • Every number comes from the setup sheet, not the AI
  • Safety steps checked against your lockout program
  • Signed and dated by the people responsible

The time moves from writing to reviewing.The knowledge stays Dale's. The sign-off stays human. This works for any job that lives in one person's head.

03

What AI actually is.

"AI" means four different things. Knowing which one someone is selling you is half the battle.

Reads and writes

AI assistants

You type or talk in plain English. It writes, summarizes, translates, and analyzes.

On the floorDale's work instruction.

Learns from data

Predictive AI

Finds patterns in sensor, production, or sales data to forecast what comes next.

On the floorFlagging a bearing before it fails.

Sees

Computer vision

Cameras plus software trained to recognize what they're looking at.

On the floorSpotting defects at line speed.

Does

Automation

Robots and software bots. Many aren't AI at all; AI helps some handle variation.

On the floorA cobot adjusting to a part out of position.

Most of what you can use tomorrow, with no new equipment, is the first kind.

1

It predicts. It doesn't know.

It writes the most likely next words based on patterns from enormous amounts of text. Likely isn't the same as true, and it isn't the same as your shop's way.

SoIt can sound sure and be wrong. Check what matters.
2

It fills in what you leave blank.

Ask a room full of people to have it draw "a bowl of fruit" and you get a room full of the same safe, generic bowl.

SoVague in, generic out. Specific in, useful out.
3

It only knows what you give it.

It doesn't know your parts, customers, machines, or procedures unless you tell it or attach them.

SoWhat you give it decides what you get.

Where it helps.

The best early wins are the paperwork around the work: repeated, written, and eating hours every week.

Production

  • Work instructions from an expert's explanation
  • Shift handoff summaries
  • Changeover and startup checklists

Quality

  • First drafts of 8D and corrective actions
  • 5 Whys and fishbone brainstorming
  • Trends in a nonconformance spreadsheet

Maintenance

  • Troubleshooting guides from manuals
  • Preventive maintenance checklists
  • Work order history summaries

Sales and office

  • Quote and RFQ reply drafts
  • Summaries of long specs and emails
  • Product and capability sheets

Purchasing

  • Side-by-side supplier comparisons
  • Plain-language contract summaries
  • Lead time and price analysis

Safety and HR

  • Toolbox talks
  • Job postings and interview questions
  • Training quizzes and translations, checked by a fluent speaker

AI drafts. People decide and sign off.

06

Use it without getting burned.

Most problems come from what goes in, and from what nobody checked on the way out.

Don't paste in

  • Customer drawings, specs, or pricing under NDA
  • Export-controlled or defense-related data
  • Employee personal or medical information
  • Passwords or system access details
  • Anything your contracts say stays inside

Always check

  • Every number: dimensions, torque, tolerances, quantities
  • Every safety step, against your actual programs
  • Names, dates, part numbers, and sources
  • Translations, with a fluent speaker
  • That it still answers what you asked
Free versus business accounts. Free accounts may use your conversations to improve their models unless you change the settings. Business plans generally offer stronger protections. Read the terms, and when in doubt, describe the situation instead of pasting the real document.

If you wouldn't email it to a stranger, don't paste it in.

You don't need a 30-page policy to start. One page that says which tools are approved, what never goes in, and that a person reviews anything leaving the building covers most of the risk.

07

Start Monday.

Four habits that get good results, and five steps to prove it on one real task.

Know what finished looks like.

Who uses the result, and what can they do with it?

Give it your context.

Who it's for, the format, your source material, your rules.

Steer it like a conversation.

"Keep this, change that." "What are you assuming?"

Watch where it's going.

A GPS keeps your destination. AI can quietly change it.

Your first five steps.

  1. Pick one annoying task.

    Repeated, written, time-consuming. A weekly report, a type of email, a missing work instruction.

  2. Write down what finished looks like.

    Who uses it, and what they'll be able to do.

  3. Try it with non-sensitive material.

    A general assistant and the four habits. Expect some back and forth.

  4. Compare it honestly.

    Faster? As good, once a person reviewed it? Be honest about both.

  5. Share it, and write the rule.

    Save the prompt that worked, show a coworker, and put your one-page guardrails in writing.

08

Prompts to keep.

Starting points for common shop tasks. Fill in the brackets, remove anything confidential, and steer from there. They work in ChatGPT, Copilot, or any assistant.

Toolbox talk

Safety and training

Write a five-minute toolbox talk on [topic] for [type of crew] at a [type of manufacturing] shop. Plain language, one real-world example, and three questions to ask the crew at the end. Don't invent statistics.

Customer email reply

Sales and office

Draft a reply to the customer email below. Our goal: [what you want to happen]. Tone: professional and direct. Don't promise dates, prices, or anything not stated in my notes. My notes: [key facts] Customer email: [paste it, with confidential details removed]

Corrective action first draft

Quality

Help me draft an 8D corrective action report for this problem: [describe the defect, where it was found, and how many parts]. Walk me through 5 Whys one question at a time before you write anything. Mark anything you're assuming.

Troubleshooting guide

Maintenance

Using only the manual section I'll paste below, create a one-page troubleshooting guide for [machine]: symptom, likely cause, what to check, and when to stop and call maintenance. If the manual doesn't cover something, say so. Manual section: [paste it]

Supplier comparison

Purchasing

Compare these supplier quotes in a table: price, lead time, minimum order, payment terms, and anything unusual. Then list the questions I should ask each supplier before deciding. Quotes: [paste the key details]

Meeting to action items

Everyone

Turn these meeting notes into a list of action items with an owner and due date for each. If an owner or date is missing, flag it instead of guessing. Notes: [paste them]

Job posting

HR and hiring

Write a job posting for a [role] at a [size and type] manufacturer in [town]. Must-haves: [list]. Nice-to-haves: [list]. Shift and pay: [details]. Plain, honest language. No buzzwords.

When the answer is generic

A follow-up for anything

What assumptions did you make that I didn't tell you? Ask me the questions you need answered to make this specific to our shop.

AI won't replace the people who know how your shop runs. It can make sure what they know gets used.
