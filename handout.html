The tools, easiest first

  1. General assistants: ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, Claude. Free to $20–25 per person per month for business plans.
  2. AI in software you own: Microsoft 365 Copilot ($21–30 per person per month on top of Microsoft 365), ERP and maintenance add-ons
  3. Built for your shop: custom assistants with your SOPs, small AI-built tools
  4. Industrial systems: vision inspection, predictive maintenance, forecasting

Four habits for good results

  1. Know what finished looks like. Who uses it, and what they can do with it.
  2. Give it context only you have. Audience, format, source material, rules.
  3. Steer it like a conversation. "Keep this, change that." "What are you assuming?"
  4. Watch where it's taking you. AI can quietly change the destination.

Where to start

  1. Pick one repeated, written, time-consuming task.
  2. Write down what finished looks like.
  3. Try it with non-sensitive material.
  4. Measure it: minutes before and after.
  5. Share what worked, and write a one-page rule.

Don't paste in

  • Give people an approved tool, or they'll use their own
  • Customer drawings, specs, or pricing under NDA
  • Export-controlled or defense-related data
  • Employee personal or medical information
  • Passwords or system access details

Always check

  • Every number: dimensions, torque, tolerances
  • Every safety step. Lockout stays in your posted procedure.
  • Names, dates, part numbers, sources
  • Translations, with a fluent speaker

Try this: turn what one person knows into a work instruction

Turn the transcript below into a one-page work instruction for [the job]. Who it's for: [who will use it and how experienced they are]. Format: numbered steps, one action per step, short sentences. Rules: If the job requires lockout or another regulated safety procedure, say so at the top and refer to that procedure; don't write those steps. Use only what's in the transcript. Don't add steps that weren't said. If anything is missing or unclear, list it under "Questions for the supervisor" instead of guessing. Transcript: [paste it here]

Record the expert, transcribe it, remove anything confidential. A person reviews and signs off.
